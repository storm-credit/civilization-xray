## S02 — Macchioni et al., “An investigation into preservation of wood from Venice foundations,” *Construction and Building Materials* 111 (2016), 652–661. DOI: 10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2016.02.144
Class: PEER_REVIEWED

Useful findings:
- samples from piles and horizontal planking in multiple Venice districts
- foundation soils described as mixtures including sand, silt, clay and peat with generally low bearing capacity
- close wooden piles used to consolidate soil beneath important buildings
- several wood species found, not a single universal species
- preservation varies with depth, element thickness/orientation and species
- decay can exist in waterlogged foundation wood
- observed diffuse decay was not simply equivalent to general building instability in the studied set

---

## S03 — Pizzo/Macchioni et al., “Assessing the wood compressive strength in pile foundations in relation to diagnostic analysis: The example of the Church of Santa Maria Maggiore, Venice,” *Construction and Building Materials* 114 (2016), 470–480. DOI: 10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2016.03.173
Class: PEER_REVIEWED / CASE STUDY

Useful findings:
- Santa Maria Maggiore foundation piles directly studied
- documents a Venetian foundation technique using densely inserted short piles, with a timber deck (`zatterone`) above in the described established palace/foundation tradition
- described purpose in that context is shallow-soil reinforcement rather than assuming every pile reaches a deep stiff stratum
- complex pile/deck treatment not universal under every part of every building; associated with important/load-bearing portions in described practice
- sampled alder piles showed variable preservation
- deeper buried samples were better preserved than shallower ones in the studied case
- residual strength can decrease even when wood remains in service

---

## S04 — Bernabei et al., “The wooden foundations of Rialto Bridge (Ponte di Rialto) in Venice: Technological characterisation and dating,” *Journal of Cultural Heritage* 36 (2019), 85–93. DOI: 10.1016/j.culher.2018.07.015
Class: PEER_REVIEWED / CASE STUDY

Useful findings:
- Rialto Bridge wooden foundation material directly studied
- footings mainly alder in studied samples and generally well preserved overall
- radiocarbon evidence shows more than one construction period in the sampled foundation history
- Rialto is a strong example that a named structure can have its own foundation history and should not be flattened into one citywide canonical story

- preservation is conditional, not magical
